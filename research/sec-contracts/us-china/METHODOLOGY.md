# U.S.–China SEC contract corpus — methodology (v0.5)

Each source is a publicly filed SEC exhibit; the agreement date is not necessarily its filing date. A named U.S. purchaser does not prove that it was the importer of record for a shipment.

## Review states

- `source-verified`: source exhibit inspected and counterparties identified; substantive clauses not yet reviewed.
- `clause-mapped`: selected provisions mapped and linked to the SEC exhibit; not comprehensive.
- `observed`: language in operative provisions supports the narrow summary.
- `observed-with-anomaly`: operative language exists but numbering/cross-references are internally inconsistent.
- `located-needs-analysis`: section located; characterization provisional.
- `not-found` should only be used after documented full-document review; never equate to absence from other controlling contracts.

## Legal-system boundaries

Contractual cost or risk allocation does not control CBP determinations. The importer of record, real shipping practices, HTS classification, tariff effective dates, origin evidence, UFLPA applicability, and other matters require independent, transaction-specific proof. Do not infer that New York governing law automatically excludes the CISG.

## Reproducibility

Preserve accession-linked `source_url`, verbatim printed clause identifiers, extraction status, any incorporated exhibits, document revision identity and human review trail. Avoid retaining confidential third-party nonpublic data.
